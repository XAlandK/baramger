Baroler · User scenario

A Marswalk with Baroler

This is the story of two walks on Mars, told from the user's side. It shows what the crew sees and does with the app, from the first screen to the last. It does not explain how the app works inside.

The people are made up. Everything they see comes from the built app: the screens, the messages in grey boxes (the app's own words), and the times and numbers from a demo run of each walk. Parts 1 to 3 follow a walk to the Bright Angel area of Jezero crater. Part 4 follows the same crew a week later, on the second walk: collecting the sample tubes at Three Forks. Times are Mars local time.

Part 1

The evening before: plan the walk

Sol 112 · inside the habitat · Plan screen

  1. 0119:30Habitat

    Open Baroler

    Sees
    The whole planet, then the map flies down to Jezero crater and the Bright Angel area. A menu at the top says Zone Z2 · Bright Angel. The side panel says Plan tomorrow's walk. Everything on this screen works without internet.
    Does
    Nora opens Baroler on the habitat wall screen. Kawa joins her.
  2. 0219:33Habitat

    Read the science goal

    Sees
    A goal card: Study the spotted rocks at Cheyava Falls, with the question Do the small dark spots in the Cheyava Falls rock hold signs of ancient life? and why people are needed: a geologist can look at the spots from many angles, compare nearby rocks, and choose the best sample.
    Does
    Nora reads it out. They agree the Cheyava Falls rock is the heart of tomorrow's walk.
  3. 0319:40Habitat

    Look at the ground in layers

    Sees
    A sharp photo map from orbit. Switches turn on more layers: Steep ground (yellow for 15–25°, take care; red for over 25°, no-go), Rock types with named colours, Rocky or dusty ground from night-time heat, and the Perseverance path. Compare side by side puts the photo on the left and the layers on the right, with a handle to slide between them.
    Does
    Nora and Kawa slide between the layers. They see red no-go patches on the steep slopes, and a band of sand ripples (the rock map calls it the eolian bedform unit) close to the habitat.
  4. 0419:55Habitat

    Look at the stops

    Sees
    Four stops are already in the plan, each where Perseverance once stood: Rover stop, sol 1159, Cheyava Falls rock, Rover stop, sol 1231 and Rover stop, sol 1254. Each stop card shows the rover's photos (What you will see), the rock type, the ground slope, the tasks and the minutes planned there. + Add stop lets them click the map to add a new stop, and arrows change the order.
    Does
    Nora opens the Cheyava Falls card: Upper etched unit, 40 minutes, four tasks (Wide photo, Close-up photos of the spots, Voice note, Take sample). She adds a crew note to it: Bring the close-up camera.
  5. 0520:05Habitat

    Find the safest route

    Sees
    After a tap on Find safest route, a line joins the stops: green is easy, yellow means take care, and it is dashed where there is no radio. It goes around the red no-go ground. The summary shows 3.20 km distance, 3 h 41 min with stops, 2 h 19 min in reserve, and the steepest part, 21°. Below are a height profile and a list of legs, such as Rover stop, sol 1231 → Rover stop, sol 1254 · 359 m · 19 min · up to 21°.
    Does
    Kawa looks for the hardest part. The 21° slope on the way to the last stop is yellow, not red, so they keep the route and plan to go slowly there.
  1. 0620:20Habitat

    Preview the walk

    Sees
    A Walk-through flies a drone-style camera along the route. The height profile shows where it is, and at each stop it pauses on the rover's photos.
    Does
    They watch it twice and note where the slope gets steep near the end.
  2. 0720:35Habitat

    Safety check and approval

    Sees
    A checklist with five green items and two amber ones. Time: 221 min planned. 139 min (39%) left in reserve; the rule is at least 30%. Amber, the steep part: About 344 m of the route is between 15 and 25 deg: take care there. Amber, the radio: 2 short stretches with no radio, the longest about 4 min (limit 15 min). Stay together there. Daylight from 07:10 to 18:03, sun activity quiet, and air between −85 and −40 °C in walk hours. The turn-back times: Planned return 12:26. Latest return that keeps the reserve: 12:57.
    Does
    They keep the start time of 08:45 and press Approve plan. The app answers Plan approved at 20:35. Copied to tablets and helmets when they dock. Kawa also saves the plan file.
Part 2

Walk day: do the walk

Sol 113 · outside · Walk tablet and HUD

  1. 0808:45Airlock

    Step outside

    Sees
    The tablet: 08:45 local Mars time · On time · Back by 12:26, and the next stop: Rover stop, sol 1159 · 1.21 km · about 30 min · E. The helmet shows the stop, 1.2 km, TIME LEFT 6:00, a radio sign, and one line: Sand ripples ahead · keep to firm ground.
    Does
    They leave the airlock and head east, keeping to firm ground around the sand ripples.
  2. 0909:10Leg 1

    A radio gap and a steep bit

    Sees
    Just before the first stop the tablet warns Radio drops ahead for about 4 min. A minute later: No radio to the habitat now. Stay together. and Steeper ground ahead (up to 20°). Slow down.
    Does
    They stay side by side and slow down. They reach the first stop at 09:15, right on time: You have arrived at Rover stop, sol 1159. Leaving it, they cross a second short gap, and the tablet counts down: Contact back in about 3 min.
  3. 1010:00Stop 2

    Arrive at the Cheyava Falls rock

    Sees
    You have arrived at Cheyava Falls rock. The stop timer starts at 0 of 40 minutes. The crew note Bring the close-up camera is at the top, then the rover's photos of this spot and the task list. Below are three buttons: Stay 10 more min, Leave now and Skip next stop.
    Does
    Nora holds the tablet up and sees the same rock in front of her. She ticks the tasks as she works. She types Dark spots also on the next boulder and taps Pin note, which pins it to this spot on the map. Kawa spots another interesting rock a few steps away and taps Mark for next walk. It pins Look at next time where they stand.
  1. 1110:56Stop 2

    Running late

    Sees
    They pressed Stay 10 more min four times, because the spots are worth it. The status went from On time to 10 min behind plan, then 30 min behind plan. Now it turns amber: Running late: back at 13:06, but the reserve rule needs you back by 12:57. Skip the next stop or leave sooner. The helmet says Running late · reserve at risk.
    Does
    Kawa taps Skip next stop. The route redraws from where they stand, straight to the last stop, and the return time drops to 12:36. They finish the sample and leave the rock at 11:20.
  1. 1211:45Leg to stop 4

    The steep part

    Sees
    On the way to the last stop the helmet shows Steep ground ahead · 21°, and the tablet Steeper ground ahead (up to 21°). Slow down. The status stays at 10 min behind plan.
    Does
    They slow down on the yellow part of the route and reach Rover stop, sol 1254 at 11:50. Its tasks: Photos up the slope and Collect a sample if safe. The ground is firm, so they take the sample.
  2. 1312:16Return

    Head home

    Sees
    The next target is now Habitat · 581 m · about 20 min. The sand-ripple warning comes back near the habitat. On the tablet map, the white line of where they really walked sits on top of the coloured plan.
    Does
    They walk back and reach the habitat at 12:36. The walk ends by itself and the tablet shows Walk complete · Back at the habitat: 3.12 km walked, 3 h 51 min outside, 3 stops visited, 1 skipped.
Part 3

After the walk: look back and share

Sol 113 · inside the habitat · Plan screen

  1. 1415:30Habitat

    Replay the walk

    Sees
    Replay the walk moves the crew marker along the white line of where they walked, over the coloured plan, with a time slider and speeds of ×60, ×120 and ×240. A list called What happened shows every moment, from 08:45 Left the habitat to 10:56 Skipped Rover stop, sol 1231 and 12:36 Back at the habitat. A Plan and reality table shows 80 of 40 min at the rock and the last stop reached +10 min late.
    Does
    Nora taps lines in the list to jump to those moments. Kawa uses the table to explain why stop 3 was skipped.
  2. 1516:00Habitat

    Send the report to Earth

    Sees
    Ready to send to Earth: 3 stops visited, 1 skipped, 3.12 km walked. Earth is 1.32 AU away today, so the report takes about 11 minutes to arrive, and a reply at least 22 minutes.
    Does
    Nora saves the report, a one-page summary with the map, the stops, the notes with their positions, the timeline, the conditions and the safety check, plus its data file, ready for the next contact with Earth.
  3. 1616:10Habitat

    Start the next plan

    Sees
    Back on the Plan screen, each stop shows how it went: visited last walk, or skipped last walk for stop 3. Under Ideas for next walk, Kawa's mark appears: Look at next time, with a button Add as stop.
    Does
    Nora taps Add as stop. The rock joins the plan for the next walk, next to skipped stop 3, and the story can start again at scene 1.
Part 4

A week later: collect the sample tubes

New walk

Sol 120–121 · Three Forks sample depot · Plan, Walk tablet and HUD

In 2022 and 2023, Perseverance left ten sealed sample tubes on the ground at Three Forks, on the flat crater floor near the old river delta. This walk brings them back to the habitat.

  1. 1719:30Habitat · Sol 120

    Switch to the other walk area

    Sees
    At the top of the map, a menu shows Zone Z2 · Bright Angel. It also offers Zone Z1 · Three Forks.
    Does
    Nora picks Three Forks. The map opens on the depot area, with the same layers and the same safety rules as before.
  2. 1819:35Habitat

    Read the goal and the depot note

    Sees
    The goal card: Collect the sample tubes at Three Forks. Ten numbered stops, from Depot spot 1 (sol 652) to Depot spot 10 (sol 686), each with four tasks: find the tube, photograph it where it lies, check it is sealed and clean, pick it up. A yellow note says each stop is where the rover parked while it dropped a tube, so the tube should lie within a few metres. It links NASA's depot map.
    Does
    Kawa opens the NASA depot map and compares it with the stops. Nora opens a few stops to see the rover's photos of the ground there.
  3. 1919:50Habitat

    Put the stops in order and find the route

    Sees
    The ten pins sit close together; zoomed out they show only their numbers, and zooming in shows each name. Find safest route shows 1.2 km · 2 h 10 min · 3 h 50 min reserve. Every check is green: steepest ground 6°, radio contact the whole way, sunrise 06:44, sunset 18:41.
    Does
    Kawa tries Suggest order. It saves only about 10 m, because the spots are so close, so they keep the order the tubes were dropped in. This walk is mostly stops, not walking. Both astronauts approve the plan.
  4. 2008:45Airlock · Sol 121

    Step outside

    Sees
    The helmet arrow points to the first depot spot, about 580 m away. The tablet shows Tubes picked up 0 of 10.
    Does
    They walk west across the flat crater floor with an empty sample carrier.
  5. 2109:00First depot spot

    The first tube

    Sees
    The tablet says You have arrived, shows the rover's photo of this spot and starts a 10-minute stop timer.
    Does
    Nora sees the tube a few steps away. She photographs it where it lies, checks the seal, and picks it up. The tablet now shows Tubes picked up 1 of 10. The next spot is only about 10 m away.
  6. 2209:50Depot spot 6

    A tube that is not right at the stop

    Sees
    At this stop there is no tube at their feet. The stop timer keeps running. Kawa remembers the note from the evening before: the tube should be within a few metres.
    Does
    They search in a small circle and find the tube 5 m to the north, with dust on top. Nora ticks the four tasks and pins a field note: Tube 6 lies 5 m north of the stop. The note goes into the report, so the next crew knows.
  1. 2310:55Return

    Home with all ten tubes

    Sees
    After the last depot spot, the helmet arrow points to Habitat · 540 m. When they reach the habitat, the walk ends by itself and the tablet shows the summary: Tubes picked up 10 of 10, 10 stops visited, none skipped.
    Does
    They hand the tube carrier to Sami for safe storage.
  2. 2414:00Habitat

    Replay and report

    Sees
    The replay shows the short walk and the time spent at each depot spot. The report lists every spot with 4 of 4 tasks, and the field note about tube 6 with its exact position.
    Does
    Nora taps Send to Earth. The app shows that the report will take about 11 minutes to reach Earth.

What the crew never has to do

The point of Baroler, from the user's side

  • Wait for the internetEverything for the walk is already on the tablet and helmet.
  • Read a technical mapColors and short messages tell them what is safe.
  • Jump between websitesPhotos, rock types and terrain are in one place.
  • Guess how steep it isThe route already avoids slopes that are too steep.
  • Lose track of timeThe app always shows when to turn back.
  • Arrive somewhere unknownA rover photo shows each stop before they get there.
  • Search blindly for a tubeEach depot stop brings them to within a few metres of it.
  • Count tubes by handThe tablet counts each tube as they pick it up.

Plan together the night before, walk with clear guidance, and come home on time with good science.
